# Websites

## Plan the page around the content

- Identify what a visitor needs to understand or find first and what they can
  do next. For a landing page, make the offer and next action easy to recognize.
  For documentation, make the current topic and its place in the navigation
  easy to scan, including when a visitor arrives through a deep link.
- Give the first view one focal point suited to the subject. It can be a title,
  a product demonstration, a strong image, or the content the visitor came
  to read. Arrange later sections around distinct questions, examples, or
  decisions. Use real copy and assets to judge the composition before polishing
  details.
- Use media with a clear purpose. Prefer project assets and choose crops that
  preserve the subject across aspect ratios. Keep essential copy as text and
  ensure it stays readable over the darkest and lightest parts of an image.
  Describe informative imagery and keep decorative imagery out of the
  accessibility tree.

## Build and review

- Let content determine breakpoints. Use fluid widths and cap reading measure
  where useful. Check narrow navigation and long or translated headings.
  Keep visible reading order aligned with document and keyboard order.
- Provide appropriately sized image sources, reserve their layout space, and
  defer below-fold media when useful. Load an image that dominates the initial
  view early and check the initial layout while media and fonts are loading.
- Open the page at wide and narrow widths. Check the first view for hierarchy,
  then scroll through the whole page to look for repetition, broken crops,
  unreadable text, or obscured anchors. Follow the primary action or use site
  navigation from a deep link, whichever best represents the visitor's task.
